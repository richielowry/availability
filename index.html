Home tuition
What is home tuition?
One-to-one teaching in your child’s own home, planned around their strengths, interests and pace. Sessions are calm, structured and individual to each child.
Who is it for?
Early years and primary school age children, including children on the autism spectrum, children with additional learning needs, children who are out of school or awaiting a school place, and children who need catch-up or help with a transition. If you are unsure whether it suits your child, get in touch and we can talk it through.
Where do you provide tuition?
In your home, across Kilkenny City and the surrounding areas. Get in touch if you are not sure whether you are in range.
Can I arrange private tuition outside the Home Tuition Grant?
Yes. Families can arrange private sessions without applying for the grant. Please contact me to discuss what your child needs and the cost.
What happens at the first meeting?
We have an introductory chat about your child, what is working, what is not, and what you would like to achieve. There is no obligation. After that I put together a personal learning plan.

I send short updates after sessions and arrange regular progress reviews.
The Home Tuition Grant
What is the Home Tuition Grant?
It is Department of Education funding towards home tuition for children who cannot attend school or are waiting for a suitable school place. It is an interim measure, so the grant stops once a suitable school placement becomes available. Parents or guardians apply to the Department, and approved hours are paid to the tutor by the Department, not by the family. The official page is on gov.ie.
Which children are eligible?
The Department sets four schemes: special educational needs and medical grounds, no school place, children in care, and maternity-related absences (the last is for post-primary students, so it does not apply to my tuition). The three that matter for early years and primary-age children are below. Tap each one for details.
1. Special educational needs and pre-school autism

Form HTSEN1. This has two parts.

Early intervention for children with autism

  • Children aged 2½ to 3 with an autism diagnosis (assessed under DSM-5 or ICD-10 criteria).
  • Children aged 3 to 4 with an autism diagnosis where an early intervention school place is not available.
  • Children over 4 with an autism diagnosis and an early intervention recommendation, who will not reach age 6 during the school year and for whom a place is not available.

For the 3 to 4 and over-4 groups, the National Council for Special Education (NCSE) must confirm that no early intervention place is available. Combined hours from State pre-school supports and home tuition are capped at 10 per week for ages 2½ to 3, and 20 per week for children over 3.

Children awaiting a special class or special school place

  • Children aged 4 and over with significant special educational needs for whom a special school or special class place is not available.
  • The NCSE assesses this through your local Special Educational Needs Organiser (SENO).
  • Up to 20 hours per week, subject to the school calendar.
2. Significant medical condition, or school phobia and anxiety

Form HTMED1.

  • Significant medical condition: an enrolled student whose condition has caused, and is likely to keep causing, major disruption to attendance. A medical report and the school’s attendance record are considered. For primary school pupils the range is 2 to 5 hours per week.
  • School phobia, anxiety or depression: only in exceptional cases, where time away from school is needed for medical or therapeutic treatment aimed at returning the child to school. This requires a report from a psychologist or psychiatrist. Home tuition cannot replace school.
3. No school place, or children in care
  • No School Place (NSP): a child who is temporarily without a school place or offer and is actively seeking one in a school recognised by the Department. The local Tusla Educational Welfare Officer (EWO) confirms the child meets the criteria and provides the form. Children are generally aged 6 to 16. Tuition is up to 9 hours per week for 12 weeks.
  • Children in Care: a child in State care who is without a school place because of an emergency placement or a disruption to their care placement. The form comes from the child’s social worker.
How do we apply, and who decides?

Parents or guardians apply to the Department of Education, and the Department decides. Other professionals complete their own section of the form, such as the SENO, the school principal, a medical practitioner or the EWO, depending on the scheme. The tutor completes a section too.

I cannot confirm whether a child is eligible, but I am happy to talk you through the process. Please wait for written approval from the Department before tuition starts.

Closing dates are set each year and differ by scheme, so check the current circular on gov.ie.

What does a tutor need to have?
  • Teaching Council registration for the whole approved tuition period.
  • Vetting through the Teaching Council before tuition begins.
  • A statutory declaration, plus a Form of Undertaking submitted with the application.

Parents, student teachers and teachers on approved leave cannot act as the tutor. I am registered with the Teaching Council (Reg. No. 343874) and Garda vetted.

Who pays the tutor under the grant?
For approved hours, the Department pays the tutor (or Tusla, where it engages the tutor) once a claim form is received. The Department does not pay for travel or preparation time, and parents should not pay a tutor directly for approved grant hours.
